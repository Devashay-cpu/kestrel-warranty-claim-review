# Phase 3 - final model, predictions and evidence

All numbers come from `artifacts/phase3/*` (generated by `scripts/run_phase3.py`) and `artifacts/phase2/*`. **No hold-out outcomes exist; nothing below is a test result.**

## 1. Final model
Regularised logistic regression (L2, C = 0.5, scikit-learn pipeline), output = probability that a claim is fraud, used as the ranking score. Saved with its preprocessing, partner history and claim log in one file: `artifacts/phase3/final_model.joblib` (loader: `kestrel/final.py`).

## 2. Features (26, unchanged from Phase 2)
- Claim: amount, amount / list price, list price, days since purchase, days to warranty expiry, near-expiry flag, photo, partner inspected, inspector-note present/type, customer's earlier claims, small-claim (< Rs 2,000), auto-approved (small and on/after 1 May 2026), hour, weekday, product family, outlet type.
- Outlet activity (no labels, earlier claims only): claims in last 7 / 30 days, share of small claims in 30 days.
- Outlet fraud history (labels known 14+ days before the claim, or frozen at 30 Jun for the hold-out): decided claims, fraud cases, same for last 90 days, smoothed fraud rate (all-time and 90 days).
- Rejected in Phase 2: outlet age, serial-number features, free-text description. Never used: claim_id, source, label, raw text, partner id.

## 3. Training data
11,142 unique labelled claims (141 fraud) from 1 Apr 2025 to 30 Jun 2026, of which 1,422 (36 fraud) are after the May 2026 rule change. Excluded: 202 undecided (blank-label) claims, 4 claims whose description contained injected instructions (treated as untrusted data, not followed). 681 resubmitted claim numbers collapsed to one row.

## 4. Weighting decision: post-May rows x5 (regime adaptation, limited evidence)
Chosen mechanically by checks written into the script before the test file was scored (all six passed):

| Scenario (fold C = June, trained with ~380 post-May rows) | Uniform PR-AUC | x5 PR-AUC | x5 minus uniform, 95% CI, P(x5 better) |
|---|---|---|---|
| C fresh history | 0.365 | 0.516 | +0.14 [-0.001, 0.31], 0.97 |
| C history 30 d older | 0.055 | 0.169 | +0.13 [0.02, 0.27], 0.997 |
| C history 60 d older | 0.055 | 0.166 | +0.12 [0.01, 0.29], 0.995 |
| Fold B (no post-May rows) | 0.023 | 0.023 | identical by construction |

x5 was also better in both halves of June (0.66 vs 0.50 and 0.37 vs 0.18) and in top-40 results (fold C fresh: precision 37.5% vs 25%, net per check +Rs 289 vs +Rs 121). Predicted probabilities are 1.45x the observed rate (uniform 1.38x), so no extra over-confidence. One coefficient sign flips between the two fits (|coef| > 0.05). **Caveats: one validation month, 22 positives, fraud concentrated at 8 outlets, and the weight was selected on that same month.** The lower end of the interval touches zero in the fresh scenario. x20 (0.578) was not used: no evidence beyond one more point on the same fold. After x5, post-May rows carry 42% of the effective weight (12.8% of rows). On the 2,252 test claims the x5 and uniform models rank very similarly (Spearman 0.91; 99 of the top 120 shared).

## 6. Expected hidden score
**Metric: PR-AUC over all 2,252 claims (accuracy only as context).** Expected **about 0.28, plausible range 0.17 to 0.52, floor near 0.03 (chance) if a new fraud wave appears at outlets with no history.** This is a range, not a point: intervals on each scenario span roughly 0.04 to 0.80, and the test fraud rate is unknown.
- Why 0.28: the hold-out claims sit 1-92 days after the 30 Jun history freeze; the three June runs with history 14-43, 44-73 and 74-103 days old scored 0.52, 0.17, 0.17; their equal-weighted mean is 0.28.
- Why not higher: partner history goes stale in weeks (0.50 -> 0.18 across June for the uniform model); fold C is where x5 was selected; fold C is one month.
- Why it could be near chance: in fold B all 36 frauds were at outlets with no known fraud and five outlets held 81% of them. 81 hold-out claims are at outlets with no decided history.
- Test shift: 77% of test claims are small and uninspected (16% in labelled training data, 51% of post-May labelled data is auto-approved small).
- Text for the submission form: "PR-AUC about 0.28 (plausible 0.17-0.52, could be near the fraud rate of ~3% if new outlets start committing fraud); accuracy is uninformative here because predicting no fraud scores 97-99%."

## 7. Test prediction summary (`predictions.csv`, 2,252 rows, validated)
Scores 0.00001-0.969, median 0.003, 99th percentile 0.69; scores sum to 71.8, i.e. the model implies roughly 50-70 frauds in the window (fraud rate ~2-3%, after allowing for its 1.2-1.45x over-prediction; an inference, not an observation). The top 40 per month (120 claims, `test_top_claims.csv`) fall at 26 outlets: 7 outlets provide 88 of 120 (SP3118 17, SP3286 16, SP3129 13, SP3160 11, SP3318 11, SP3319 10, SP3232 10); 94% are auto-approved small claims, median Rs 1,426; 79% are at outlets with known fraud. Each carries up to three plain-language reasons.

## 8-9. Top-K economics and expected rupee impact (hold-out, 3 months, 2,252 claims, ~751/month)
Extrapolated from validation precision and rupees per check (not test outcomes); 40 reviews/month = 120 reviews = 5.3% of claims; goodwill Rs 380 per genuine claim held.
| Scenario | Frauds caught of 120 | Fraud Rs stopped | Goodwill cost | **Net** | per check | per month | Net if Rs 260 contact cost also applies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pessimistic (fold B) | 1.5 | 2,967 | 45,030 | **-42,063** | -351 | -14,021 | -73,263 |
| Central (mean of three C runs) | 35 | 51,377 | 32,300 | **+19,077** | +159 | +6,359 | -12,123 |
| Optimistic (C fresh) | 45 | 63,120 | 28,500 | **+34,620** | +289 | +11,540 | +3,420 |
Ranking and decision are separate. Ranking says which 40 to look at first. Decision: check a claim only when expected value p x amount - (1-p) x 380 is positive (break-even p = 380 / (amount + 380); a Rs 800 claim needs p > 32%). Only 92 of the 2,252 claims clear that bar (67 with the Rs 260 contact cost), fewer than the 120 slots; use fewer reviews rather than fill the slots. A high score is a reason to investigate, never to reject. Whether the Rs 260 contact cost applies per investigated claim is an assumption in the policy text; it decides whether the central case is positive.
Break-even for the desk: with fraud claims averaging about Rs 1,500, checks pay for themselves above about 20% precision (about 34% if the Rs 260 applies).

## 10. Main failure risks
1. New fraud outlets with no history (fold B): near chance, 79 of 80 flags wrong. 2. Stale history: needs monthly refresh with fresh investigation outcomes. 3. Cold start: 81 claims, and any outlet onboarded after June. 4. Selection optimism on x5 and on fold C. 5. Fraud concentration: 7 outlets drive the queue, so one outlet changing behaviour moves the result. 6. Label noise: undecided cases dropped, legacy undecided exported as 0. 7. Over-confident probabilities (1.15-1.45x) under regime change. 8. Coefficients on correlated history counts are unstable, so reasons are shown per group, not per coefficient.

## 11. Data / regime limits
141 positives (36 after May); 1 May 2026 rule change moved fraud into small uninspected claims (3.2% vs 0.5% before); folds B and C overlap in time; LAG_DAYS = 14, EB strength 20 and the Rs 260 reading are assumptions; the pack has no resolution timestamps, so the legacy UTC issue could not be checked.
Observation, not modelled: among post-May small claims, those priced Rs 1,950-1,999 (just under the auto-approve limit) were fraud in 10 of 99 cases (10.1%) versus about 2.5% for other small claims. Worth a rule or feature next; it is only 10 cases.

## 12. Monitor after deployment
Per month: precision of the 40 reviewed (alarm below 20%), Rs stopped per check, share of reviewed claims at unseen outlets, concentration by outlet, score calibration (mean score vs investigated fraud rate), blank-label rate and investigation lag, fraud rate of claims just under Rs 2,000, and retrain monthly with fresh labels.
